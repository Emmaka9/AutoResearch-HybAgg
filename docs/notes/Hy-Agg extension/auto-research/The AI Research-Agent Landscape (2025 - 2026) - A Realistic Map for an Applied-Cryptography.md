
## TL;DR

- **There is no tool that does "everything" credibly for cryptography/systems research.** The honest state of the art is a chain of strong _point solutions_ — literature agents (FutureHouse Crow/Falcon/Owl), ML-experiment agents (AI Scientist-v2, MLE-STAR, AIDE), and general coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, OpenHands) — that you orchestrate yourself. "End-to-end" systems (Sakana's AI Scientist, Zochi, Carl, FutureHouse's Robin) have produced real, peer-reviewed artifacts, but only in ML or wet-lab biology with cheap, fast, scalar feedback loops — not in formal-crypto/HE/systems work.
- **For Hyb-Agg specifically, automate the mechanical majority of the grunt work:** reimplementing baselines (SecAgg, LightSecAgg, xMK-CKKS) and writing OpenFHE/C++ glue with coding agents, running client/dimension benchmark sweeps and energy logging on the Pi, generating plots/tables, drafting related-work and boilerplate sections, and self-critique passes. **Keep human:** novel protocol design, the threat model, and the security proof — AI assistance for cryptographic proof assistants (EasyCrypt, Tamarin) is documented as weak today.
- **The tool names you saw are mostly real:** "auto-research-claw" = **AutoResearchClaw** (open-source 23-stage pipeline, `aiming-lab`); "meta-claw" = **MetaClaw** (same lab; an agent "skill-learning" proxy); "skydiscover" = **SkyDiscover** (UC Berkeley Sky Lab, an AlphaEvolve-style evolutionary code-discovery framework); "flywheel" is generic (a data/eval-loop pattern, plus several unrelated products). Treat the "claw" projects as early, lightly-evidenced community software, not validated systems.

## Key Findings

1. **Best-documented successes are narrow.** Sakana's AI Scientist-v2 had one of three papers accepted at an ICLR 2025 workshop (avg score 6.33, ~top 45%); Intology's Zochi and Autoscience's Carl reached workshop/ACL acceptances; Google's Co-Scientist and FutureHouse's Robin produced lab-validated biology hypotheses. **All are in ML or biology**, where experiments are fast, cheap, and scored by a single metric — the opposite of crypto-protocol research.
    
2. **On rigorous benchmarks, agents are useful but sub-human at real research.** On OpenAI's PaperBench, the best agent (Claude 3.5 Sonnet New, open-source scaffolding) reached a 21.0% average replication score, and o1 reached 26.6% on a 3-paper subset, versus a human ML-PhD baseline of 41.4% after 48 hours of effort. MLE-bench: best setup wins Kaggle medals in ~16.9% of competitions at one attempt (rising to ~34% at eight attempts). RE-Bench: agents beat humans 4× at a 2-hour budget but humans overtake them by 8 hours and double them at 32 hours. The pattern: **agents are fast and cheap on short, well-specified tasks and degrade on long-horizon, ambiguous ones** — exactly the regime of novel protocol design.
    
3. **Coding agents are the highest-ROI tool for you, but specialized crypto C++ is their weak spot.** On SWE-bench Verified, top scaffolds (OpenHands ~72%, mini-SWE-agent + Claude 4.5-/GPT-5-class models 70%+) are strong. But the only FHE-specific benchmark, TFHE-Coder (arXiv:2503.12217), shows off-the-shelf models invoke irrelevant or non-existent FHE library APIs and need RAG + compiler-in-the-loop scaffolding to be usable. Expect to supply OpenFHE docs/examples as context.
    
4. **Idea generation: provocative but unproven.** A Stanford study (Si, Yang & Hashimoto, arXiv:2409.04109) found LLM-generated ideas were judged "more novel (p < 0.05) than human expert ideas while being judged slightly weaker on feasibility." But the same group's follow-up (arXiv:2506.20803), in which 43 experts each spent 100+ hours executing assigned ideas, found that novelty edge **disappears after execution**. Idea agents are brainstorming partners, not discovery engines.
    
5. **Formal-methods AI help is immature for crypto.** No EasyCrypt-specific LLM tooling exists; the main LLM+Tamarin benchmark (CryptoFormalEval, arXiv:2411.13627) reports essentially cautionary/negative results. Proof assistants remain a human job.
    

## Details

### A. The landscape by research stage

**Idea / hypothesis generation.** The headline academic result is Si, Yang & Hashimoto (Stanford), _Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas?_ (arXiv:2409.04109; ICLR 2025), which recruited 100+ NLP researchers (49 wrote baseline ideas; 79 reviewed) and found LLM ideas judged more novel (p<0.05) than expert ideas but slightly weaker on feasibility. The crucial caveat is their 2025 follow-up, _The Ideation–Execution Gap_ (arXiv:2506.20803), where 43 experts each spent over 100 hours implementing an assigned idea and wrote a 4-page paper; the LLM ideas' scores dropped significantly on all metrics (novelty, excitement, effectiveness, overall; p<0.05), closing — and partially reversing — the gap. The authors also note LLMs lack diversity (duplicate ideas when scaled) and cannot reliably self-evaluate. **ResearchAgent** (Baek et al., KAIST/Microsoft, NAACL 2025; arXiv:2404.07738) is a concrete system: it starts from a core paper, expands via an academic citation graph plus an entity-based knowledge store, and iteratively refines problem/method/experiment with multiple LLM "reviewing agents" aligned to human-elicited criteria. Google's **AI Co-Scientist** (Gemini-based) and **Nova** are other examples; Co-Scientist uses a multi-agent Generation/Reflection/Ranking/Evolution/Proximity/Meta-review tournament.

**Literature review / related-work / citation finding.** This is the most mature and lowest-risk category. **FutureHouse** offers Crow (fast cited Q&A, built on the open-source PaperQA2), Falcon (deep multi-source synthesis with access to specialized databases such as OpenTargets), and Owl (formerly HasAnyone — "has anyone done X?" prior-work detection). FutureHouse reports these beat frontier search models and exceed PhD-level precision in head-to-head literature search; PaperQA2 was reported as achieving superhuman accuracy on some literature-QA tasks and is Apache-2.0 open source. Commercial/academic peers include Elicit, Consensus, Undermind, and Semantic Scholar tooling. **Caveat:** hallucinated citations are a documented, worsening problem — a 2026 arXiv audit by researchers at Cornell, UCLA, UC Berkeley and Tsinghua examined 111 million references across 2.5 million papers and estimated 146,932 hallucinated citations in 2025 papers (hallucination rates as of Aug 2025: 0.39% arXiv, 1.91% SSRN, 0.27% PubMed Central, 0.21% bioRxiv) — so always verify against the cited source. Grounded, passage-citing tools (PaperQA2-style) mitigate but do not eliminate this.

**Experiment design.** Mostly bundled into the end-to-end systems (AI Scientist, Agent Laboratory, Co-Scientist) rather than offered standalone. These propose experimental plans, but Si et al. and others note LLM research _plans_ are often infeasible without human vetting.

**Autonomous experimentation & coding agents (your core interest).**

- _Research-specific ML agents:_ **AIDE** (Weco AI) is the scaffold that, with o1-preview, set the original MLE-bench result; **MLE-STAR** (Google Research, arXiv:2506.15692) uses web search to seed solutions plus targeted, ablation-guided code-block refinement — it improved AIDE's any-medal rate on MLE-bench-Lite from 25.8% to 43.9% (with ~36% gold) and its above-median rate from 39.4% to 63.6% (note: some press reports loosely state "won 63% of competitions," conflating the above-median rate with medals — the medal rate is 43.9%). **Agent Laboratory** (Schmidgall et al., Johns Hopkins/AMD; arXiv:2501.04227) coordinates "PhD/postdoc" LLM agents through Literature Review → Experimentation (mle-solver) → Report Writing (paper-solver), with co-pilot and autonomous modes; **AgentRxiv** (arXiv:2503.18102) extends it with a shared preprint server so parallel agent-labs build on each other (reported 13.7% relative improvement on MATH-500 from collaboration).
- _General coding agents (most relevant to your OpenFHE/C++ pipeline):_ **Claude Code** (Anthropic), **OpenAI Codex / Codex CLI**, **OpenHands** (formerly OpenDevin; ~72% SWE-bench Verified, 100+ LLM backends, Docker sandbox), **Devin** (Cognition; ~45.8% SWE-bench Verified unassisted), **Google Jules**, **Cursor** agents, and **Aider** (open source, terminal-based). Top mini-SWE-agent setups with Claude 4.5-/GPT-5-class models now exceed 70% on SWE-bench Verified. These are the workhorses for reimplementation, refactoring, test generation, and benchmarking harness code.

**Data analysis & visualization.** FutureHouse **Finch** (biology data analysis); the data-analysis stages of AI Scientist/Agent Laboratory; general coding agents with a notebook/REPL. Strong for plotting and standard statistics; weak at choosing the _right_ statistical framing.

**Paper writing / drafting.** AI Scientist-v2 and Zochi/Carl produce full LaTeX manuscripts including figures; AI Scientist-v2 added a vision-language feedback loop to refine figures. Documented failure modes: hallucinated results/ablation tables, wrong LaTeX figure references, mis-comparison of numbers, and citation errors (the accepted Sakana paper misattributed LSTM origins).

**Peer review / self-critique.** Built into most systems as an "AI reviewer" or "critic agent" (Co-Scientist's meta-review; Virtual Lab's Scientific Critic; AI Scientist's reviewer). Google is piloting agentic review tools (Paper Assistant Tool and ScholarPeer) with ICML/NeurIPS/STOC. Evidence that LLMs can _reliably_ evaluate research quality is weak (Si et al.: "LLMs cannot evaluate ideas reliably").

### B. The specific named systems

- **Sakana AI — The AI Scientist / AI Scientist-v2** (open source). v2 (arXiv:2504.08066) uses agentic tree search managed by an experiment-manager agent and removed v1's hand-written templates. Milestone: one of three submissions was accepted at the ICLR 2025 "I Can't Believe It's Not Better" (ICBINB) workshop (scores 6/7/6, avg 6.33, ~top 45%), the first fully AI-generated paper to pass peer review — but Sakana withdrew it post-review and admitted none of the three met their bar for an ICLR _conference-track_ paper. An independent evaluation (arXiv:2502.14297) found weak literature reviews, roughly half of experiments failing, and hallucinated results.
- **Google AI Co-Scientist** (Gemini; not open source; access via Gemini Enterprise / trusted-tester program, with DOE National Lab access announced). Multi-agent hypothesis tournament. Validated wins: AML drug-repurposing candidates confirmed in vitro; a liver-fibrosis candidate (Vorinostat) reduced a TGFβ-induced chromatin change by 91% (published in _Advanced Science_); an antimicrobial-resistance gene-transfer mechanism that matched unpublished experimental findings at Imperial College. A May 2026 _Nature_ paper documents these. **All wet-lab biology.**
- **Agent Laboratory / AgentRxiv** (Schmidgall et al., JHU/AMD; open source). See above.
- **Stanford Virtual Lab** (Swanson, Wu, Bulaong, Pak, Zou; _Nature_, July 2025; open source). An AI PI agent leads specialist agents (e.g., immunologist, ML, computational biologist, and a Scientific Critic) with ~1% human input; designed 92 nanobody candidates against SARS-CoV-2, two showing experimentally validated binding to the JN.1 variant while retaining binding to the original Wuhan strain.
- **ResearchAgent** — see idea-generation above.
- **Zochi / Intology** (commercial; beta). Claims first AI system whose paper ("Tempest: Automatic Multi-Turn Jailbreaking of Large Language Models with Tree Search") was accepted to the _main_ proceedings of ACL 2025 (top ~8.2%, meta-review 4/5); earlier ICLR 2025 workshop acceptances. Humans supplied only the research domain ("novel jailbreaking methods").
- **Carl / Autoscience** (commercial; raised $14M, March 2026, led by General Catalyst). First AI-authored work accepted at ICLR 2025 workshops (3/4 acceptance, Tiny Papers then a full-length workshop track — "Investigating Alignment Signals in Initial Token Representations"); papers voluntarily withdrawn pending community norms. Now pivoting to a managed service that auto-improves enterprise ML models.
- **FutureHouse tools** (nonprofit; Eric Schmidt-backed; commercial spinout **Edison Scientific** raised $70M Dec 2025 at a ~$250M valuation). Crow, Falcon, Owl (literature), Phoenix (chemistry; explicitly error-prone/experimental, a redeployment of ChemCrow), Finch (data), **Aviary** (agent-environment/gym training toolkit), **PaperQA2** (open source). **Robin** is their orchestration workflow that chained Crow+Falcon+Finch to identify ripasudil (a ROCK inhibitor) as a candidate dry-AMD treatment — their "first end-to-end AI-driven discovery," though the wet-lab experiments were run by humans. **Kosmos** (Edison Scientific) is described as their most advanced autonomous-research system.
- **Coding agents:** Codex/Codex CLI, Claude Code, Devin, OpenHands, Jules, Cursor, Aider — see above.
- **ML benchmarks:** **MLE-bench** (OpenAI, arXiv:2410.07095; 75 Kaggle competitions; o1-preview+AIDE ~16.9% medals at pass@1, ~34% at eight attempts). **RE-Bench** (METR, arXiv:2411.15114; 7 ML R&D environments; 71 attempts by 61 experts; agents 4× humans at 2h, humans ~2× agents at 32h). **PaperBench** (OpenAI, arXiv:2504.01848; replicate 20 ICML 2024 Spotlight/Oral papers from scratch; 8,316 gradable sub-tasks; best agent Claude 3.5 Sonnet New 21.0%, o1 26.6% on a 3-paper subset, human ML PhDs 41.4% after 48h; GPT-5 later reported as top scorer in its system card). **MLAgentBench**, **DSBench**, **CORE-Bench** (reproducibility) round out the set.

### C. The mystery names — what they actually are

- **"auto-research-claw" → AutoResearchClaw** (GitHub `aiming-lab/AutoResearchClaw`): an open-source "fully autonomous & self-evolving research from idea to paper" pipeline with **23 stages**, multi-agent debate, a Docker experiment sandbox, an AI-slop/NeurIPS-checklist audit, domain-specialist execution agents (high-energy physics, biology, statistics), and an "ARC-Bench" of 55 open-ended research topics. **Evidence base is self-reported** (its own benchmark is an authored simulation); treat as promising but unvalidated. Note: the SEO blog "Auto Research Claw" (juliangoldie.com) is marketing content, not a neutral source.
- **"meta-claw" → MetaClaw** (GitHub `aiming-lab/MetaClaw`): a "talk to your agent — it learns and evolves" proxy that sits in front of any OpenAI-compatible agent (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini, Kimi, etc.) and meta-learns reusable "skills." Its companion paper (arXiv:2603.17187) reports skill-injection improving AutoResearchClaw's composite robustness ~18.3% — but the authors state the gains are specific to an authored simulation benchmark and may not transfer to production workloads.
- **"Dr-Claw"** (GitHub `OpenLAIR/dr-claw`): a related "research IDE" that auto-discovers Claude Code/Codex/Gemini sessions and organizes Survey/Ideation/Experiment/Publication directories — relevant as an orchestration shell.
- **"skydiscover" → SkyDiscover** (UC Berkeley Sky Lab; open source `skydiscover-ai/skydiscover`): a modular framework for **LLM-driven evolutionary algorithm discovery** (an open AlphaEvolve/OpenEvolve/GEPA-style system) across 200+ optimization tasks, decomposed into Context Builder / Solution Generator / Evaluator / Solution Selector, with new AdaEvolve and EvoX algorithms. This is the most directly relevant "mystery" tool for a systems/algorithms researcher: it's a clean harness for _benchmark-driven, evolutionary code optimization_.
- **"flywheel"** is **not a single product** — it's the generic "data/eval flywheel" pattern (log → evaluate → retrain/refine → improve), used by NVIDIA NeMo, Galileo, Intercom's Fin, and others. There is also an unrelated ETL product (flywheeletl.io) and an "Agent Flywheel" collection of Claude Code tooling. Don't anchor on it as a specific research tool.

### D. End-to-end autonomy — critical assessment

"End-to-end" claims are real but bounded. Sakana, Zochi, Carl, Virtual Lab, and Robin each automated a _full loop_ and produced an externally recognized artifact — but every case sits in a domain with **cheap, fast, scalar feedback** (an ML metric, a binding assay, a tournament score). Crucially: (1) most acceptances are workshop/Tiny-Paper tier with 60–70% acceptance rates, and several were withdrawn; (2) the one main-conference acceptance (Zochi/ACL) is on an NLP topic; (3) wet-lab "discoveries" still required humans to run the physical experiments. For cryptography — where "experiments" are correctness/security proofs and the failure cost is a broken protocol — **no end-to-end system is demonstrated, and the benchmark data (PaperBench 21% agent vs 41.4% human; RE-Bench's long-horizon human advantage) predicts they would underperform.** The defensible posture: compose point solutions, keep humans on the critical path.

### E. Orchestration architecture — how to actually build your loop

Your mental model (agents in a loop with explicit objectives and success benchmarks, iterating) is exactly right and matches what works (MLE-STAR's ablation-guided refinement; AI Scientist-v2's tree search; SkyDiscover's evolutionary loop). Best practice:

1. **Make the reward verifiable.** The systems that work best optimize against an objective, machine-checkable signal: a compiling-and-passing test suite, a correctness oracle, a latency/throughput/energy number. For Hyb-Agg you already have this — correctness checks against a reference, and benchmark metrics (time vs #clients, vs model dimension, Pi energy). Encode them as the agent's score function. Avoid LLM-judged rewards for anything security-critical.
2. **Pick a framework by control needs.** **LangGraph** (graph/state-machine, built-in checkpointing with time-travel, human-in-the-loop, durable execution; reached v1.0 in late 2025) is the safest pick for an auditable iterative research loop. **CrewAI** is fastest for a role-based prototype (PI/implementer/reviewer crew) but carries up to ~3× the token overhead on simple flows. **AutoGen / AG2** (now folding into the unified Microsoft Agent Framework, GA April 2026) suits debate/critique patterns. **OpenAI Agents SDK** (explicit handoffs; OpenAI-locked; replaced the experimental Swarm) and **Google ADK** (hierarchical agent tree; Gemini-tuned; native A2A protocol) are the vendor options. Note framework choice can swing benchmark performance by up to ~30 points and token cost substantially on identical models (per Princeton HAL data cited in 2026 surveys), so keep the orchestration layer thin.
3. **Use checkpoints + human gates.** Insert human approval at: threat-model/spec definition, choice of baselines, and before accepting any security claim. LangGraph's checkpointing is designed for exactly this.
4. **Chain specialized tools, don't seek a monolith.** Realistic pipeline: FutureHouse Falcon/Owl (related work) → you define spec/threat model → Claude Code/OpenHands (implement baselines + harness in OpenFHE/C++) → scripted benchmark sweeps on the Pi (deterministic, logged) → coding agent for plots/tables → drafting agent for boilerplate sections → AI-reviewer/self-critique pass → human verification of everything security-relevant. Protocols like **MCP** (tool connection) and **A2A** (cross-framework agent calls), now under Linux Foundation stewardship, make this composition easier.
5. **Instrument and iterate (the legitimate "flywheel").** Log every run/trace, evaluate against your metrics, and feed failures back as reusable lessons/skills (this is what MetaClaw automates, and what NVIDIA's "data flywheel" formalizes).

### F. Concrete application to Hyb-Agg — realistic division of labor

**Strongly automatable today (use coding + literature agents):**

- _Reimplementing baselines_ (SecAgg, LightSecAgg, SASH, xMK-CKKS) on matched Pi hardware. Coding agents excel at translating a paper's pseudocode/reference repo into runnable code; supply the original repos as context. Verify correctness against published test vectors.
- _Benchmark sweeps and energy measurement._ Generating parameterized harnesses (vary #clients, model dimension), orchestrating runs, parsing logs, computing confidence intervals, and producing matplotlib/pgfplots figures and LaTeX tables — ideal agent work because the output is checkable and deterministic.
- _Ablation studies_ (e.g., ECDH-masking on/off, packing/batching strategies) — agents can enumerate and run the grid once you specify it.
- _Literature positioning / related-work._ Falcon/Owl/Crow (or Elicit/Undermind) to find and summarize SecAgg-family papers and detect prior art — but verify every citation (hallucination risk above).
- _Drafting_ system/implementation/experimental-setup sections and figure captions from your results; a first-pass self-critique against a checklist.

**Partially automatable, with heavy supervision:**

- _OpenFHE/C++ implementation of the protocol itself._ Coding agents help with scaffolding, build files, serialization, and glue, but the TFHE-Coder benchmark (arXiv:2503.12217) shows models hallucinate FHE library APIs and need RAG over the actual OpenFHE docs/examples plus a compile-test loop; the authors found "significant limitations in off-the-shelf models, while agentic optimizations — such as retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and few-shot prompting — reduce errors." Treat agent output as a junior contributor's PR: useful, but every crypto-relevant line gets human review (parameter selection, noise budget, RNS/CRT handling, side-channel and serialization correctness are easy to get subtly wrong).
- _Data interpretation_ — agents summarize; you decide what the numbers mean for the security/performance trade-off.

**Keep human (do not delegate):**

- _Novel protocol design_ — combining MK-CKKS + ECDH masking into a new secure-aggregation scheme is precisely the long-horizon, low-feedback creative work where agents are weakest (RE-Bench's long-horizon human advantage; the ideation–execution gap).
- _Threat-model and security-definition decisions_ — what adversary, what collusion bound, what leakage is tolerable. Wrong here = the whole result is wrong.
- _The security proof._ This is the clearest "stay human" zone. There is **no EasyCrypt-specific LLM tooling**, and the main LLM+Tamarin benchmark, **CryptoFormalEval** (Apart Research, arXiv:2411.13627), reports cautionary/largely negative results — frontier models (GPT-4o, o1-preview, Claude 3.5 Sonnet) struggle with Tamarin's syntax and formal-verification concepts (e.g., misplacing observables, producing invalid syntax), with "no model achieving perfect scores" and reliable autonomous vulnerability detection rare. LLM theorem-proving progress is concentrated in Lean/Isabelle/Coq/Dafny on math and general software (e.g., ~38% proof coverage reported on FSCQ; up to ~58% on Dafny MBPP-derived methods), not cryptographic game-based proofs. Use an LLM at most as a brainstorming aid for proof structure, then write and machine-check the proof yourself in EasyCrypt.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Adopt low-risk, high-ROI tools now.**

- Use a literature agent (FutureHouse Falcon/Owl or Crow, or Elicit/Undermind) for related-work and prior-art sweeps. **Benchmark for keeping it:** >80% of returned citations verify to real, on-point papers on a 20-citation spot check. If the hallucination rate is higher, downgrade to grounded PaperQA2-style tools only.
- Adopt one coding agent (Claude Code, or OpenHands for open-source/self-hosted control) for baseline reimplementation, harness code, plotting, and tests. **Benchmark:** the agent-produced baseline reproduces published numbers within reported tolerance and passes your correctness oracle.

**Stage 2 — Build a thin, benchmark-driven loop.**

- Wrap your existing Pi benchmark pipeline as the verifiable reward. Use **LangGraph** to orchestrate an implement→build→test→measure→refine loop with a human-approval node before any change touching crypto correctness. Consider **SkyDiscover** if you want to _evolutionarily optimize_ a well-defined, machine-scored sub-component (e.g., a packing/batching strategy or a parameter set under fixed security constraints) rather than design the protocol.
- **Threshold to escalate autonomy:** only widen the agent's unattended scope to a new task type after it has cleared that task type's correctness oracle unaided on ≥5 prior runs.

**Stage 3 — Use end-to-end systems only as exploratory aides.**

- You may trial AI Scientist-v2 / Agent Laboratory / AutoResearchClaw on a _non-security_ sub-question (e.g., an empirical performance study) to see the full loop — but do not route security claims through them. **Stop signal:** if an end-to-end run produces a hallucinated table or unverifiable citation (check the first run), restrict it to drafting only.

**Always:** verify every citation; treat all generated crypto code as unreviewed; write and machine-check proofs yourself; log runs to build your own improvement flywheel.

## Caveats

- **Hype vs evidence:** Vendor and SEO claims (especially the "claw" marketing blogs and Intology/Autoscience press) outrun peer-reviewed evidence. The robust, peer-reviewed data points are PaperBench, MLE-bench, RE-Bench, the Stanford ideation studies, and the _Nature_/_Advanced Science_ biology validations.
- **Domain transfer is not established:** Every documented end-to-end success is in ML or biology with fast scalar feedback. Generalization to formal cryptography is unproven and, by the benchmark evidence, unlikely in the near term.
- **Moving target:** Model/agent capability is changing fast (the GPT-5-class and Claude 4.5-class results cited here are from late-2025/2026 sources); re-check benchmark leaderboards before committing to a specific agent.
- **Some figures are secondary:** A few quantities (framework cost/performance multipliers, some effect-size summaries) come from secondary surveys; verify against primary sources before quoting precise numbers in your own work. The MLE-STAR "63%" figure circulating in press is the above-median rate, not the medal rate (43.9%).
- **Unverified/early software:** AutoResearchClaw, MetaClaw, and Dr-Claw are early community projects whose performance claims rest on self-authored benchmarks; SkyDiscover and PaperQA2 are better-documented. The "flywheel" name maps to no single research product.
